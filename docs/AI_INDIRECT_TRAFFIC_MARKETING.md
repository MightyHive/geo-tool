# AI Indirect Traffic — explainer for marketing leaders

A plain-language guide to what the model measures, how it works, and how to read the results. No technical background required.

---

## In one sentence

**AI Indirect Traffic** estimates how the rise of AI chatbots has changed your website’s normal search (SEO) and Direct visits — not the visits already labelled as coming from ChatGPT and similar tools.

---

## Two different AI numbers (do not mix them up)

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct AI traffic** (in analytics) | Visits Google Analytics already attributes to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and similar. Counted, not modelled. |
| **AI Indirect Traffic** (this model) | Estimated change in **SEO** and **Direct** visits linked to AI becoming more common in the market. |

Someone can discover your brand in ChatGPT and still arrive via Google or by typing your URL. Analytics may never mark that visit as “AI.” This model is built for that second, harder-to-see effect.

---

## How the model works (simple version)

### The question it asks

For recent weeks on your site:

> If AI chatbots had stayed as small as they were before they took off, how many SEO and Direct visits would you likely have had — compared with what you actually got?

The gap between those two stories is your **AI Indirect Traffic** estimate.

### How it answers that

1. The current model studied **61 web properties** over several years of weekly traffic. Twenty app properties were excluded because app analytics cannot reliably identify AI referrals.
2. We measured how big AI chatbots became across that whole group (a market “AI is growing” signal).
3. We learnt how sites like yours typically respond — shops, service businesses, or publishers each tend to behave differently.
4. We allow for other common explanations of traffic change: brand size, gradual growth or decline, seasonality, and how often people search for your brand name.
5. For your site, we apply the pattern for your business type to **your actual** SEO and Direct traffic.

When you first run the estimate, you get a quick answer based on similar businesses. Once we have enough of your own history (about eight good weeks of analytics plus brand search data), the estimate can be refined with your site included more fully.

### What it deliberately does not do

- It does **not** invent sales or conversion impact. Purchases and conversion rate shown nearby are what analytics already recorded.
- It does **not** replace your Direct AI channel report. Use both: one for visible AI referrals, one for knock-on effects on SEO and Direct.
- It is **not** a perfect experiment. It is the best estimate from patterns across many sites. Other market shifts that moved in line with AI can still influence the answer.

## How to read the results

### Actual vs “without AI growth”

Charts typically show:

- **Actual** — what really happened (SEO or Direct sessions).
- **Without AI growth** (sometimes called counterfactual) — the model’s estimate of what those same weeks would have looked like if AI chatbots had stayed small.

| Pattern | Plain reading |
| --- | --- |
| Actual **above** “without AI growth” | AI growth is associated with **more** SEO or Direct visits than you’d otherwise expect. |
| Actual **below** “without AI growth” | AI growth is associated with **fewer** SEO or Direct visits (substitution / displacement). |
| Lines close together / wide uncertainty band | Effect is small or unclear — treat as “too early to call.” |

### The headline numbers

For each channel (SEO and Direct) you usually see:

1. **Central estimate** — best single guess for how many visits were gained or lost over the recent window (often about the last 13 weeks).
2. **Range** — a confidence band. We are reasonably sure the true figure sits somewhere in this range.
3. **Robust vs uncertain** — a reliability flag (see below).

**Positive** = more visits linked to AI growth.  
**Negative** = fewer visits linked to AI growth.

Read SEO and Direct separately. One can be up while the other is down.

### Robust vs “tail-sensitive / uncertain”

| Label | What to do with it |
| --- | --- |
| **Robust** | Direction is clear and holds up when we check an alternative version of the AI signal. Safe to use in planning conversations. |
| **Tail-sensitive / uncertain** | The answer depends on a few extreme weeks, or the range still includes “no change.” Use as directional only; do not treat as a firm KPI. |

### What “business type” means for you

Your site is scored like similar businesses:

- **Retail** — shops and product sites  
- **Services** — quote / booking / lead-gen style businesses  
- **Publishers** — content and media sites  

A new site starts with the typical pattern for its group. That is intentional: with little history, guessing a unique private effect would be less reliable.

## How marketing should use this

**Use it to:**

- Separate “AI sent us traffic we can see in analytics” from “AI changed our classic search and Direct channels.”
- Brief leadership on whether AI looks like a **tailwind**, a **headwind**, or **not yet clear** for SEO and Direct.
- Prioritise GEO / content / brand work where Indirect impact is negative or uncertain and Direct AI is still small.
- Track direction over time as more weeks of data arrive and estimates move from “similar businesses” to “more site-specific.”

**Do not use it to:**

- Set a single “AI ROI” sales number (purchases are not modelled).
- Blame or credit one campaign week in isolation (the model looks at patterns over many weeks).
- Replace channel reporting for ChatGPT / Perplexity / Gemini referrals.

### A practical reading checklist

1. Look at **Direct AI** in analytics — how much visible AI referral traffic do we already get?  
2. Look at **AI Indirect** for SEO — up, down, or unclear? Is it marked robust?  
3. Look at **AI Indirect** for Direct — same questions.  
4. Tell the story in one line, for example:  
   - “Visible AI referrals are growing, and SEO still looks supported.”  
   - “Visible AI is small, but SEO Indirect looks down — classic search may be getting displaced.”  
   - “Indirect estimates are uncertain — watch the next quarter before changing budget.”

---

## Bottom line

AI Indirect Traffic answers: **as AI chatbots grew, did our normal search and Direct visits rise or fall relative to what we’d expect?**

Read the central number for size, the range for uncertainty, and the robust flag before you act. Pair it with your Direct AI channel report — together they give both the traffic you can already see and the traffic effect that is harder to see.
